# Evidence and rehearsal

Keep three categories separate:

| Category | Examples | What it cannot establish |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanical evidence | Hash, word-like count, missing required line, broken beat reference | Whether the joke is funny |
| Editorial hypothesis | Setup gives away the turn; ending seems out of character | How this audience actually responds |
| Observed performance | Timed reading, documented viewer confusion, real audience laughter | Universal quality or causality from one show |

## A fair comparison

Preserve the original and compare it with a revision under the same brief.
Describe what changed and what should improve. Listen with comparable interpretation,
level, and pace. If one version uses excellent acting and another a flat synthetic
read, separate the writing comparison from the performance comparison.

Use blind A/B labels when practical, and record presentation order. Seeing both
versions changes expectation; repeated exposure can destroy a surprise. Different
matched groups or counterbalanced order can reduce that problem, but a small pilot
still supplies exploratory feedback rather than a statistically established win.

Ask specific questions: Where did you lose the thread? Which word changed the
meaning? Was the reaction understandable? Which version sounded like this speaker?
Keep disagreement instead of averaging it into a precise-looking 'humor score'.

## Observation records

The local tool accepts hand-annotated intervals in seconds for stable beat IDs.
Record the source as live_audience, human_read, synthetic_rehearsal, or edited_track.
Only live_audience retains a reported laughter duration. Do not relabel edited
laughter or an imagined audience to make a result appear tested.

Document audience context and known confounders in notes: room size, microphone
placement, performer, previous exposure, topical familiarity, and competing sounds.
A loud laugh can come from recognition, a gesture, or a previous line. The next
line is not necessarily the cause. Detector thresholds and alignment errors need
human review.

ASR checks the recognized text. It does not certify intention, breath, a subtle
pause, comic emphasis, lip sync, or an audience response. A waveform and a contact
sheet are useful aids; neither replaces listening and real-time viewing.

## Select and record

Use a short decision such as keep, revise, or try in rehearsal, with the reason and
the exact line/version. A no-change decision is a valid editorial result. Never
claim that the toolkit improved audience response unless that response was actually
observed and the limits of the comparison are described.
